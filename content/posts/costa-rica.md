  Costa Rica sells itself on "pura vida" — but for parents, the real draw is that it delivers genuine adventure (zip lines, rainforest hikes, wildlife) in a country with excellent infrastructure, English widely spoken in tourist areas, and a long track record of family tourism.

  ## Structuring the Itinerary

  Avoid trying to see too much — Costa Rica's roads are scenic but slow. A common and effective plan is a few days in the Arenal volcano region (hot springs, easy hikes) followed by a few days on the Pacific coast at Manuel Antonio or Tamarindo for beach time.

  ## Choosing Activities by Age

  Many adventure operators offer age- and height-based options: reduced-speed zip lines, tandem lines for toddlers riding with a guide, and gentle river floats instead of whitewater rafting. Always ask about minimum ages and weight limits before booking.

  ## Health and Comfort

  Pack reef-safe sunscreen, a rain jacket for sudden downpours, and quick-dry clothing. Air conditioning isn't universal at eco-lodges, so check in advance if that matters for nap schedules.
