Tokyo has a reputation for being overwhelming, but for families it's actually one of the easiest big cities to navigate. The trains run on time, bathrooms are spotless and stocked with changing tables, and nearly every neighborhood has a park within a five-minute walk.

## Getting Around

Buy a Suica or Pasmo IC card for the whole family at the airport — you tap in and out of trains and buses without fumbling for tickets, which is a lifesaver when you're also managing a stroller and a diaper bag. Elevators exist at nearly every station entrance, though they can be tucked in a corner, so budget a few extra minutes when transferring lines.

## Where to Stay

Shinjuku and Asakusa both work well for families — Shinjuku puts you near Shinjuku Gyoen (a huge park perfect for burning off jet lag energy), while Asakusa is quieter, more traditional, and closer to the Tokyo Skytree.

## A Sample Day

Start slow. Jet lag hits kids hard, so mornings are for parks and shrines (Meiji Shrine's forested approach is stroller-friendly and calm), afternoons for one big activity, and evenings for konbini snack runs and an early bedtime. Don't over-schedule — Tokyo rewards families who leave gaps in the plan for wandering.
