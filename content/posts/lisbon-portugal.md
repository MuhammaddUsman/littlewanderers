  Lisbon punches well above its price point for a European city break. Flights and hotels are cheaper than most Western European capitals, but the sights — castles, trams, a working waterfront, and pastel-colored buildings — feel every bit as grand.

  ## Managing the Hills

  The Alfama and Bairro Alto districts are beautiful but steep. Base yourself in the flatter Baixa or Chiado neighborhoods and use funiculars (the Elevador da Glória, for instance) to handle elevation changes without exhausting little legs.

  ## A Slower Pace Works Best

  Portuguese meal culture is unhurried, which pairs well with families — nobody rushes you out after the main course, so there's time for kids to finish (or not finish) their plates without stress. Lunch is typically the biggest meal, and many restaurants offer a "prato do dia" (dish of the day) that's affordable and simple.

  ## Day Trip Option

  Sintra, 40 minutes by train, adds fairy-tale palaces to the itinerary, but its hills are steeper than Lisbon's — plan it as a single day with a carrier rather than a stroller.
