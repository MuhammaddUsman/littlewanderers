content: |
  Paris has a reputation as an adults' city, but it has quietly become one of the best-organized cities in Europe for families — many major museums have dedicated children's trails, activity booklets, and shorter timed-entry kid tickets.

  ## Where to Stay

  The 5th, 6th, and 7th arrondissements put you within walking distance of the Jardin du Luxembourg and the Eiffel Tower, and they're generally quieter and more residential in the evenings than the busier tourist core.

  ## Pace Yourself

  Pick one major sight per day rather than stacking two or three — lines at the Louvre or Eiffel Tower alone can eat a morning. Book timed-entry tickets online in advance for every major sight to avoid standing in line with tired kids.

  ## The Metro vs. Walking

  Central Paris is compact and very walkable, and many families find pushing a stroller above ground easier than navigating stairs at metro stations. Buses, unlike the metro, are step-free and offer a scenic way to cover longer distances.
