  Orlando is less a destination than a system — one built, refined over decades, specifically around making travel with young kids manageable. Rider swap, stroller rental, mobile ordering, and genie-style planning apps all exist because families are the primary customer.

  ## Picking Your Parks

  Don't try to do Disney and Universal justice in the same short trip. A family with kids under 8 gets the most value focusing on Magic Kingdom and EPCOT; families with elementary and older kids often prefer splitting time between Universal and Magic Kingdom.

  ## Managing Crowds and Heat

  Arrive at rope drop (park opening) for the first two hours, retreat to the pool or hotel from roughly 1-4pm, and return in the evening when the heat breaks and crowds often thin slightly. This rhythm also happens to align well with toddler nap schedules.

  ## Budgeting Realistically

  Tickets, especially multi-day/multi-park passes, are the biggest cost. Off-property hotels and vacation rentals with a kitchen can meaningfully reduce food costs compared to eating every meal inside the parks.
