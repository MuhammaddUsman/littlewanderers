New York rewards families who resist the urge to check off every landmark in one trip. The city is dense and vertical in a way that tires kids out faster than the map suggests — a few well-chosen neighborhoods beat a scattershot tour of all five boroughs.

## Picking a Base

Staying near Central Park (Upper West Side or Midtown) puts you within walking distance of the park, several major museums, and manageable subway access to everything else. It's usually calmer in the evenings than Times Square-adjacent hotels.

## Managing the Scale

Group sights by neighborhood rather than by "must-see" ranking — the Museum of Natural History, Central Park, and the Children's Museum are all walkable from each other, while the 9/11 Memorial and Statue of Liberty ferry are a separate downtown day entirely.

## Getting Around

The subway is the fastest way to move, but buses are step-free and let kids watch the city go by, which matters more than speed on a vacation day. Yellow cabs and rideshares are plentiful for tired-kid emergencies, though traffic can make them slower than the subway at rush hour.
